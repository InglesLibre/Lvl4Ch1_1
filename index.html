Special Occasions

Invitations, Attire & Compliments

A2-B1 Level

Aims:

  • Talk about special occasions
  • Extend and respond to invitations
  • Give and respond to compliments
  • Use passive voice, reported speech, and conditionals with modals
Slide 2

Warm-Up: Special Occasions

Work in pairs. Discuss these questions.

  1. What special occasions do you celebrate with your family?
  2. What do you usually wear to a wedding? A birthday party? A job interview?
  3. Who do you invite to your celebrations?
  4. When was the last time you received an invitation? What was it for?

Vocabulary: wedding, birthday, anniversary, graduation, engagement, dinner party, celebration, guest, host, invitation

Vocabulary: Attire

Semi-formal: blazer, dress shirt, skirt and blouse

Casual: jeans, t-shirt, sundress, shorts

Slide 5

Listening: Describing an Occasion

Listen to the description and answer the questions. (Teacher reads or plays audio.)

  1. What was the occasion?
  2. Where was it held?
  3. What did the guests wear?
  4. What food was served?
  5. What did people do to celebrate?

Tip: Listen for key words: wedding, reception, guests, dinner, music, speeches

Slide 6

Grammar Discovery: Passive Voice

Read these sentences from invitations and announcements.

  • "The party will be held on July 4th."
  • "The wedding was organized by the bride's family."
  • "All guests are invited to the reception."
  • "The dinner will be served at 8pm."

Discuss in pairs:

  • Who is doing the action in each sentence?
  • What is the structure of the verb? (be + past participle)
  • Why do we use passive voice in invitations?

Grammar Discovery: Reported Speech

Read these sentences. What did the people say?

  • "Julie said she had a previous engagement."
  • "He told me he would come to the party."
  • "She said she was feeling unwell."
  • "They told us they had already eaten."

Discuss in pairs:

  • What changes happen to the verb tense?
  • What changes happen to pronouns?
  • What is the difference between said and told?

Slide 10

Grammar Discovery: Present Conditional with Modals

Read these sentences about invitations and attire.

  • "If the invitation says casual, you can dress comfortably."
  • "If it rains, the ceremony will be moved indoors."
  • "If you are late, you may miss the opening speech."
  • "If they invite us, we should bring a gift."

Discuss in pairs:

  • What is the structure of these sentences? (If + present simple, modal + infinitive)
  • What do the modals mean? (can = possibility, may = permission/possibility, should = advice, will = certainty)

Slide 12

Speaking: Invitations

Useful Phrases:

Extending

  • "Would you like to...?"
  • "I'd like to invite you to..."
  • "Please join us for..."

Accepting

  • "I'd love to!"
  • "Thank you, I'll be there."
  • "I'd be delighted to."

Declining

  • "I'm afraid I can't..."
  • "I'm sorry, I have another engagement."
  • "Thank you, but I'm busy."

Role Play: Invite your partner to a special occasion. Use the phrases above.

Slide 13

Giving and Responding to Compliments

Giving Compliments

  • "You look stunning in that dress!"
  • "That's a gorgeous outfit."
  • "That's a striking necklace!"
  • "You look fabulous."
  • "That was a wonderful dinner!"
  • "Your ring is magnificent."

Responding

  • "Thank you!"
  • "That's so kind of you!"
  • "Thank you, I got it at..."
  • "You're very sweet."
  • "I'm glad you like it!"

Practice: Give a compliment to your partner. Your partner should respond.

Slide 14

Speaking: Invitations & Compliments

Role Play: Work in pairs. Use the scenarios below.

Scenario 1

Invite your friend to a wedding. Describe the wedding. Give a compliment about your friend's outfit.

Scenario 2

Invite your partner to a birthday party. Tell them what to wear. Give a compliment about their cooking.

Scenario 3

Invite your colleague to a dinner party. Describe the food. Give a compliment about their work.

Use grammar: Passive ("The party will be held..."), Reported speech ("She said she would come...")

Writing: Invitation Email

Task: Write an email inviting a friend to a special occasion.

Include:

  • The occasion and date
  • The location
  • What to wear
  • A request to RSVP
  • Use at least 2 passive voice sentences
  • Use 1 reported speech sentence

Useful phrases:

"I would like to invite you to..." / "The party will be held on..." / "Please let me know if you can come."

Slide 17

Error Correction and Reflection

Common Errors to Avoid:

  • Passive: Incorrect: "The party will hold on July 4th." → Correct: "The party will be held on July 4th."
  • Reported Speech: Incorrect: "She said she has a previous engagement." → Correct: "She said she had a previous engagement."
  • Conditionals: Incorrect: "If the invitation says casual, you can't dress comfortably." → Correct: "If the invitation says casual, you can dress comfortably."

Self-Check Questions:

  • Can I use passive voice to describe events and invitations?
  • Can I report what someone said using reported speech?
  • Can I give advice or make suggestions using conditionals with modals?
  • Can I extend and respond to invitations politely?
  • Can I give and respond to compliments?
Slide 18

Special Occasions - Teacher Notes

Aims

  • Talk about special occasions
  • Extend and respond to invitations
  • Give and respond to compliments
  • Use passive voice, reported speech, and conditionals with modals

Timing

180 minutes (3 hours) with breaks

Procedure

  1. Introduction and Warm-Up (15 min): Discuss special occasions in pairs. Elicit answers.
  2. Vocabulary: Occasions (10 min): Match activity. Check answers.
  3. Vocabulary: Attire (10 min): Discuss different dress codes. Practice describing outfits.
  4. Listening (12 min): Listen to the description and answer questions.
  5. Passive Voice Discovery & Practice (20 min): Discover the structure. Complete gap-fill.
  6. Reported Speech Discovery & Practice (20 min): Discover the structure. Complete gap-fill.
  7. Conditionals Discovery & Practice (20 min): Discover the structure. Complete gap-fill.
  8. Break (15 min)
  9. Invitations Speaking (15 min): Practice extending and responding to invitations.
  10. Compliments (10 min): Learn phrases for giving and responding to compliments.
  11. Invitations & Compliments Role Play (15 min): Scenarios practice.
  12. Writing: Invitation Email (12 min): Students write an invitation email.
  13. Reading (10 min): Read the invitation and answer comprehension questions.
  14. Error Correction (8 min): Review common errors. Self-check questions.

Answers

Occasions Matching: 1-wedding, 2-birthday party, 3-anniversary, 4-graduation, 5-engagement party, 6-dinner party, 7-retirement party

Passive Voice: 1-will be held, 2-was organized, 3-are invited, 4-will be served, 5-was given, 6-have been sent

Reported Speech: 1-she had, 2-he would, 3-she was, 4-they had already, 5-he could, 6-she wanted

Conditionals: 1-can, 2-may, 3-should, 4-can, 5-may, 6-should

Checking Questions

  • What is passive voice?
  • When do we use reported speech?
  • What modals can we use in conditionals?

Likely Errors

  • Forgetting the auxiliary verb in passive voice (be)
  • Not changing the verb tense in reported speech
  • Using the wrong modal in conditionals

Extension Ideas

  • Write a thank-you email after a party
  • Design an invitation card
  • Role play a conversation at a party giving compliments
